Revoking UPDATE and DELETE does not make a table append-only. TRUNCATE is a separate privilege and is not covered by DELETE; ALTER TABLE, DROP TABLE and ALTER TABLE ... DISABLE TRIGGER are not covered either. An account that can drop or disable the trigger required by M21-26 can then make silent changes with no audit row at all. Amend M21-31 and align its two twins (NFR-105, DB-04) rather than replacing M21-31 wholesale. Reuse the document's own established two-layer pattern from M8-50 and DB-25 instead of inventing a new custody regime.

M21-31 [MUST]: "The audit trail shall be immutable: no user, no role, and specifically not the Administrator, shall be able to edit or delete an audit entry. Immutability shall be enforced in two layers, as for issued reports in M8-50. First, the application's database account shall hold INSERT and SELECT only on the audit tables; UPDATE, DELETE, TRUNCATE and all schema-changing rights (ALTER, DROP, and the right to create, alter, disable or drop a trigger) shall be revoked from it, and the audit tables shall not be owned by the application account. Acceptance check: as the application account, attempt in turn an UPDATE, a DELETE, a TRUNCATE, an ALTER TABLE, a DROP TABLE and an ALTER TABLE ... Acceptance check: inspect the switch register and the configuration screen; no audit-disabling entry exists. Add M21-31b [MUST]: "A daily audit-integrity job shall verify that every required audit trigger exists and is enabled, that the audit table privileges are as M21-31 requires, and that the audit trail has no gap: each audit row shall carry the SHA-256 fingerprint of the preceding row's key fields, forming a chain that the job re-computes, so that a removed or altered row is detectable even where a partition has been archived under M21-38. QUOTE VERIFIED. The text is exact, at SPEC.md:4594 and parts/section_4_functional_support.md:1669.

THE CORE CONTRADICTION IS REAL AND CONFIRMED. M21-72 claims that after pseudonymisation "the person is no longer identifiable", and its acceptance check requires a tester to confirm that "their reports remain complete and printable, and their name is gone." Those two halves cannot both pass, because the customer's name is not only in the customer master:
- M8-05 (SPEC.md:2428): the report carries the customer's name and contact information as a frozen snapshot at issue, not read live from the master.
- SPEC.md:2539-2540: the report record itself holds "Customer name (frozen)" and "Customer address (frozen)", explicitly "Read-only after issue". So the name persists in the database row, not merely inside a PDF.

So a tester executing the check would open the frozen report, see the name, and the check fails. Worse, a developer trying to make it pass would have to alter a frozen file, violating three other [MUST]s. Two MUSTs whose acceptance checks are mutually unsatisfiable is a genuine defect.

1. The correct legal position is ALREADY stated plainly in the document. M18.4 rule 5 (SPEC.md:3960) says: "A customer asks for their data to be erased while a report is retained. Technical records and issued reports are retained under a legal and regulatory obligation. Marketing and optional contact data are not... and must say so plainly (see M21)." This matches the research direction verbatim in parts/research_nabl_17025.md:646 ("technical records and issued reports are retained under a legal/regulatory obligation... Say so in the spec"). So a grievance would not in fact be answered wrongly by a reader of the whole document, and the underlying design decision in M21-72 is exactly the one the research brief recommends (parts/research_india_commercial.md:668). The defect is one overreaching clause plus a broken acceptance check, not a wrong architecture.
2. The reviewer's supporting claim about registers is FALSE. M20-18 requires that "A register print shall reflect the data as at the time of printing", and M20-01/M20-20 render the customer field as the pseudonym when identity is masked. The M20 registers are live views over the data, not frozen files, so a print taken after pseudonymisation shows the token, not the name. The name does NOT survive in the registers as the reviewer asserts.

THE PROPOSED FIX IS WRONG IN THREE PLACES and must not be applied as written:
(a) The replacement acceptance check asserts the "sample inward, test, report-issue and dispatch registers ... are unchanged and still contain the name recorded at the time of issue." That directly contradicts M20-18 and M20-20 and would silently invent a new requirement to freeze names into registers.
(b) The new retention row "Retention period of the document containing it (8-10 years)" contradicts the existing row "Sample inward, test, report issue and dispatch registers | Permanent", and also the "Life of the instrument plus 10 years" and "Audit trail" rows. It is also redundant: the table already has rows for reports, registers, financial documents and the audit trail.
(c) The fix says pseudonymisation "applies only to the live customer master" but ignores M22.1 (SPEC.md:4625), which makes CloudZoo ERP the master of customer legal name and address, with the LIMS holding a read-only copy refreshed by "a nightly full reconciliation". Pseudonymising the LIMS copy would be overwritten the same night. Without an ERP-side rule the fix is unimplementable.

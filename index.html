The Silk Testing Laboratory System

A system that knows where every sample is, who tested it, on which balance, in what room conditions, against which version of which method — and can prove all of it four years later.

How to read this

Read it top to bottom in about twenty minutes, or jump straight to Follow one lot to see the whole thing working on a real consignment. The last section, Over to you, is the only part that needs anything from you. Nothing here is technical, and nothing here is decided without you.

Where we are now

What is built, and what is waiting on you

Work has started. Rather than beginning with the easy parts, we began with the two pieces that were genuinely in doubt — because if either had turned out to be impossible, it would have been far better to find out now than a year from now. Both are settled.

✓

The certificate itself

Working

A certificate can now be produced that carries Telugu, Hindi and English correctly, is digitally signed, and can be checked years later. The signature is invisible on the page, as the accessibility standard requires, and the text can be searched and copied out — so a customer can find their own name in it rather than looking at a picture of one.

This is the thing the whole system exists to produce, and the hardest part to change once certificates have been issued. It had to be first.

!

A problem found by building it

Discovered

The standard software for putting text into a certificate crashes outright on Telugu — on ordinary words, including శ్రీ, the honorific in a great many Indian names. Not wrong output: it stops. A second piece of software now does that work instead.

No amount of design would have found this. Only running it did. A test now checks every release that the original defect is still there, so the workaround is removed the day it is fixed rather than living on forever.

✎

The calculations and the grade

Needs your tables

The arithmetic is built — averages and deviation from the individual skein weights, maximum deviation from the coarsest and finest, low neatness from the worst fifth of panels, conditioned size with the 11 per cent allowance, and the two-stage grade where the major tests set the grade and an auxiliary test can pull it down by one class.

But it will not produce a grade yet, and that is deliberate. The grade tables — the actual limit values for 4A, 3A, 2A and the rest — were not available to us, and we refused to invent them. A grade that looks right and is wrong is the worst thing this system could produce, because money is settled against it and nobody can tell by looking.

…

Everything else

Specified, not yet built

The register, the workflow, the calibration and competency checks, the billing and the portal are all written down in full — 1,291 numbered requirements — but not yet built.

The order is deliberate: prove the risky parts, then build the ordinary ones.

One decision has changed since the first draftThe earlier draft assumed this system would sit alongside a separate business software package that would hold the customer list, raise the invoices and keep the stock. The unit runs no such package, so that assumption has been withdrawn. This system now stands on its own — it keeps its own customer records, raises and numbers its own invoices and receipts, and holds its own consumable stock and equipment records. Nothing else has to be bought or installed before it can run. The accounts wing keeps the general ledger exactly as it does today; if the Board ever adopts an accounting package, a connection to it is already described and can be switched on then.
By the numbers

The figures that shaped every design decision

Nothing here is invented. Each number comes from the laboratory's own published figures or from the standards its work is governed by, and each one changed something about how the system is built.

11,294
Samples tested in 2021-22
A screen that takes ninety seconds per sample cannot get through eleven thousand samples in a year, so fast batch entry of many lots of the same test is the first design rule, not a refinement.
98%
Share that is the Limited Test
About 11,071 of those samples were the single ₹50 Limited Test, done in daily batches of ten to thirty lots, so that one path is the path the whole system is shaped around.
₹67
Average revenue per sample
Revenue of ₹7,56,789 spread across 11,294 samples means clerical minutes cost more than the test earns, which is why the re-typing and re-totalling has to go before anything else.
7
Entries in the accredited scope
Only one of the seven is raw silk (Raw Silk Yarn / Count / IS 15090 Part 5) and five are fabric tests, so most of what the unit sells falls outside the scope and the system must keep the accreditation symbol off those reports.
80
Sizing skeins weighed per lot
For silk of 3.8 tex and above, eight sizing skeins of 112.5 m are reeled from each of ten bobbins and each is weighed on its own — forty for finer counts — so one lot's size deviation is eighty readings, not one.
20
Seriplane panels, three tests
The same twenty panels wound from ten bobbins serve evenness, cleanness and neatness, so one physical piece has to be able to carry several results instead of being written down three times.
11%
Official regain for silk
Conditioned mass is oven-dry mass plus eleven per cent, and because money changes hands on that final kilogram figure the constant is held as dated reference data rather than fixed inside the program.
27 ± 2 °C
Indian standard testing atmosphere
With 65 ± 2 per cent relative humidity, this is the condition that winding, size, seriplane, serigraph and cohesion all require, so the room reading is captured beside the result and not left to memory.
24 hours
Pre-conditioning before a physical test
The specimen must reach moisture equilibrium before it can be touched, which is why waiting time is counted separately from working time whenever the system reports turnaround.
Follow one lot

From the counter to a signed certificate

Most days the Dharmavaram counter takes in Limited Test lots by the dozen — winding, wrap-reeling, kilcha, denier, one deviation figure, fifty rupees. This is the longer journey, because it exercises everything: one full BIS grading lot, from the moment a broker sets two bales down on the counter to the moment a buyer in the market scans the code on the certificate and satisfies himself it is real. Every step below is the corrected workflow of the specification, walked through by one real consignment.

The consignment in this exampleOne BIS grading lot — 2 bales, 47 books, about 120 kg of multi-end reeled raw silk marked SLS-118, declared 20/22 denier, brought in for M/s Sri Lakshmi Silks, Regatipalli, by their broker Sri M. Naidu on Wednesday 19 August 2026.
1

The request is taken

Front Desk clerk

Sri M. Naidu sets down two bales and says they are for Sri Lakshmi Silks. The clerk finds the mill by its mobile number rather than typing a name that already exists in the register in three spellings, and records Naidu separately as the sender. She writes the request: one lot, marked SLS-118, declared 20/22 denier, multi-end reeled, full BIS grading, balance of the lot to be returned, normal priority.

What the system doesIt keeps the paying customer and the man who carried the bales in two different places, and labels every declared figure "declared by customer, not verified by the laboratory". It will not let the request be confirmed until the customer's consent is on record — a signed slip, a one-time password to the registered mobile, or an attached letter.
The mistake it preventsThe certificate can only ever be issued in the name of the mill that owns the silk, never in the broker's name, because the two were never allowed to share one field.
2

The request is reviewed

Unit Incharge

Before a single skein is touched, the Unit Incharge opens the request and confirms, test by test, that the laboratory can actually do what has been asked: the method is live, an instrument of each type is in calibration, somebody is authorised for it, and there is capacity this week. He records the outcome as Accept.

What the system doesIt runs that check itself and shows a red line against any test whose only instrument is out of calibration, or for which nobody holds a current authorisation. Accepting anyway is still possible, but only with a typed justification that stays on the record for good.
The mistake it preventsNo silk may be taken in, and no work started, against a request that has not been reviewed and accepted — the receipt screen refuses and names the missing review.
3

The bales are received

Sample Receipt Clerk

The two bales come across the counter at 10:40. The clerk records the date and time, the mode of receipt, the sender, 47 books and the gross weight. She works down the condition checklist — packing intact, seal intact, dry, marks legible, quantity matching the declaration. On bale 2 the wrapping is torn open along one edge and the seal is broken.

What the system doesIt records the weight together with the identity of the balance used and whether that balance's calibration was valid at that moment. It gives the lot its laboratory number, DVM/2026-27/RS/01204, on an acknowledgement slip — no label is printed yet, because the lot has not been accepted yet.
The mistake it preventsThe torn packing cannot be saved as a note alone: any adverse answer on the checklist demands a photograph with the sample number visible in the frame, which is the laboratory's own protection if this lot is ever disputed.
4

A doubt is settled

Sample Receipt Clerk, then Front Desk with the Unit Incharge

The clerk puts the lot into Doubt Raised, which stops everything. The Front Desk telephones Sri K. Ramesh at the mill, explains that bale 2's wrapping is torn and its seal broken, and records his instruction: proceed. The Unit Incharge accepts the lot with a recorded reservation, naming the tests a damaged wrapping could affect — and that same text will print as a caveat on the certificate.

What the system doesIt blocks all testing until the consultation is on record: the date, who spoke to whom, what was put to the customer and what the customer instructed. On acceptance it prints the lot's label, carrying the sample number, the due dates and the test short codes — and nowhere on it, the customer's name.
The mistake it preventsA lot cannot leave Doubt Raised on somebody's memory of a phone call. Without the recorded instruction, no work can begin at all.
5

One order, nine tests

System

The order is one line on the rate card — Raw silk testing and grading, BIS, ₹400. The moment the lot is accepted, the system opens nine separate pieces of work against it: visual and tactual examination, winding, size deviation and maximum deviation, conditioned size, evenness, cleanness, neatness, tenacity and elongation, and cohesion. Each carries its own state, its own tester, its own instrument and its own due date.

What the system doesIt computes each due date from the moment of acceptance, counted in working hours from the laboratory's own calendar, and adds the method's mandatory 24 hours of pre-conditioning before it quotes any date to the customer.
The mistake it preventsA promised date can never be quoted that quietly ignores the 24 hours the silk must sit in the conditioning room. The wait is part of the arithmetic, not something a person has to remember to allow for.
6

Specimens drawn and conditioned

Tester / Technical Assistant24 hours

Ten skeins are drawn from the lot under the method's own draw rule and go into the conditioning room at 15:10 on the 19th, at 27 °C and 65 per cent relative humidity. They come out at 15:10 on the 20th. This is a genuine wait, not an administrative one.

What the system doesIt writes a conditioning register entry — start, end, which room, and the temperature and humidity through the period, with the source of every reading stated, whether sensor, logger export or the wall hygrometer. Each specimen keeps a permanent link back to the skein, the book and the bale it came from.
The mistake it preventsThe Start button on every conditioning-dependent test is refused until the 24 hours have actually elapsed, and it shows the earliest time work may begin.
7

A balance is refused

Section Head

The size work goes to Smt. P. Sujatha, who is authorised for IS 15090 (Part 5). The Section Head selects the quadrant balance DVM/EQ/021 for the weighing planned on the 21st. The system refuses it: that balance's calibration runs out on the 20th. He picks the other one, and the refusal stays on the record.

What the system doesIt checks competence and calibration against the planned test date, not against today — which is precisely the case a wall chart misses. It also checks that the named person is authorised for that method revision on that date, not merely that she holds the Tester role.
The mistake it preventsOnly the Unit Incharge may override either refusal, only with a reason from the controlled list, and the override opens a nonconformity by itself — so a gate that was overridden can never pass unnoticed.
8

Winding onto ten bobbins

Assigned Testerabout 70 minutes of machine time

The ten conditioned skeins are wound at fixed speed. Four threads break, and the tester records each break with its cause — two loose ends, one nib, one split. The winding leaves ten bobbins, and those ten bobbins are now the physical basis for nearly everything that follows: forty sizing skeins of 450 metres, twenty seriplane panels, ten test pieces for cohesion.

What the system doesIt records that the same ten bobbins feed the size, evenness, tenacity and cohesion work, and that the same twenty panels serve evenness, cleanness and neatness — so a later question about what was consumed, and by which test, has an answer.
The mistake it preventsA break cannot be entered as a bare number. Each one needs its cause, because the standard requires break causes and their frequency to be stated on the certificate.
9

Forty sizing skeins weighed

Assigned Testertwenty readings in under 90 seconds is the target, so roughly three minutes of typing for forty, apart from the weighing itself

Four sizing skeins of 450 metres are reeled from each of the ten bobbins, and each of the forty is weighed on its own. At around 21 denier a 450-metre skein weighs a little over a gram. On skein 27 the tester's finger slips and 10.7 goes in where 1.07 was meant.

What the system doesIt keeps forty individual readings, never an average in their place, each tied to the one sizing skein it came from and each carrying the balance used, the time, the person, and the temperature and humidity of the room. The running average and coefficient of variation update as he types, so 10.7 makes the average jump on the screen at once and raises a plausibility warning he must answer.
The mistake it preventsThe forty readings must resolve to four sizing skeins from each of ten bobbins. Forty readings that came from nine bobbins are refused at submission, with the mismatch named.
10

Twenty panels are judged

Assigned Tester

Two panels are wound from each bobbin, twenty in all, and read against the official standard photographs. Stripes are counted and sorted into evenness I, II and III. Cleanness defects are counted by class — on this lot, one major and six minor. Each panel is then rated for neatness.

What the system doesCleanness is worked out from the weighted penalties rather than in the tester's head: one major at 0.4 per cent and six minors at 0.1 per cent each leaves 99.0 per cent. Low neatness is taken as the mean of the worst four panels of the twenty, because that is what the method says — not the mean of all twenty.
The mistake it preventsNeatness is chosen from the standard photograph set — 100, 90, 80, 70, 60, 50, 30, 10 — and from nothing else. An 85 cannot be typed, because no such standard photograph exists.
11

Conditioned size in the oven

Assigned Tester

All forty sizing skeins go into the oven at 140 °C for the conditioned size. They are dried, weighed, dried again and weighed again. The conditioned size comes out at 21.34 denier against a marked 20/22 — a difference of roughly one and a half per cent.

What the system doesIt applies the official 11 per cent regain to the oven-dry mass itself, and it watches the drying: the run ends only when two successive weighings differ by no more than a quarter of one per cent. It then compares the conditioned size against the size marked on the bales.
The mistake it preventsHad that difference exceeded 7 per cent either way, the system would have discarded every other test on this lot, created the repeat work automatically, and refused to let a certificate be built from the discarded run — which is what IS 15090 requires and what is easiest to overlook at four in the afternoon.
12

The numbers lock

System, then the Assigned Tester

Nobody types an average. From the forty weighings the system produces the average size of 21.34 denier, the size deviation of 1.12 denier, the maximum deviation of 2.8 denier taken from the four coarsest and four finest, and a coefficient of variation of 5.2 per cent. Cohesion comes out in whole strokes. The grade is computed — 3A from the major tests, lowered one class by the winding shortfall among the auxiliary tests, overall 2A. The tester presses Submit.

What the system doesIt shows the formula beside each figure, keeps every intermediate value, and prints the degrading arithmetic step by step, so a grade can be argued about with evidence rather than asserted. Rounding follows the method: two decimals for size deviation in denier, one for maximum deviation, whole numbers for cohesion strokes.
The mistake it preventsAfter Submit the tester cannot change a reading. A correction is a fresh entry with a mandatory reason, and the original stays visible beside it — the same discipline as striking through, initialling and dating in the register, but impossible to skip.
13

Cohesion is sent back

Verifier — Sri B. Srinivas, Technical Assistant

The verifier works down a checklist already half-filled in for him: readings complete, balance in calibration at the time of test, method revision active, humidity in band, pre-conditioning done. On cohesion he stops. The tester's own note records a nib on test piece 6, and the method requires test pieces to be taken from portions free of such defects. He sends cohesion back with the reason "specimen preparation questionable". It is re-prepared, re-run that afternoon, and verified.

What the system doesIt re-computes every figure from the raw readings on demand and blocks verification outright if anything comes out differently from what was stored. It counts the send-back against first-time-right, and it does not stop the clock, because a re-run is not extra time earned.
The mistake it preventsThe verifier has exactly two buttons — Verify, and Send back with a reason. He cannot touch a reading; if he could, the reading would no longer belong to the person who observed it. By default he must also not be the man who did the work, and where three staff make that impossible the exception is dated, justified and counted rather than silent.
14

The certificate is compiled

Report Writer

The nine verified results are compiled into a draft Grading Certificate, and every printed value is frozen at that moment. The draft carries a diagonal DRAFT — NOT FOR ISSUE watermark, no number, no code, no signature. The bale-2 caveat appears exactly as it was written at receipt, naming the results it may affect.

What the system doesIt checks the document against the accredited scope. Certificate NABLT0726AD18713 covers exactly seven entries, and only one of them is raw silk — Raw Silk Yarn / Count / IS 15090 (Part 5). Evenness, cleanness, neatness, cohesion, tenacity and winding all sit outside it.
The mistake it preventsThe system refuses to place the accreditation symbol or a Unique Laboratory Report number on a certificate carrying even one out-of-scope result, and it names the results responsible. Whether the count figure inside a grading order may be reported separately as accredited work is a question the specification deliberately leaves open, for the Quality Manager to answer in writing.
15

Authorised and signed

Unit Incharge, as Approving Authority

He opens the draft, satisfies himself of it, and signs. The document becomes DVM/GC/2026-27/00014, dated 25 August 2026, and can never again be edited by anyone, himself included — a mistake found later is corrected by an amendment or a replacement, never by a quiet change.

What the system doesIn one action it confirms he is a declared signatory for every parameter on the document, allots the number, renders the file, takes its fingerprint, creates the verification code and the six-digit access code, and stores the exact bytes that were issued. A reprint in 2031 is the same file, letter for letter, whatever has happened since to the template or the letterhead.
The mistake it preventsSigning demands his password again at the moment of signing — being logged in is not signing — and it is refused outright if he is not a declared signatory for even one parameter on the document.
16

Invoice and release

Accounts

The invoice is raised for the work actually done, at the rate in force on the request date: ₹400 for the BIS grading, plus tax. It is DVM/I/2627/00468. Sri Ramesh pays at the counter and takes the money receipt, and the despatch of the certificate is logged with the mode, the date and who received it.

What the system doesIt bills only tests that reached Verified — anything aborted through no fault of the customer never reaches the invoice at all. Whether an unpaid certificate is held back is a setting per unit, and the specification records honestly that the unit has not yet said how it should be set.
The mistake it preventsA payment hold can block the issue of the document and nothing else. The verifier still verifies and the signatory still signs, because an unpaid bill must never be able to lean on a technical decision.
17

A buyer scans the code

Anyone holding the paper

A fortnight later a buyer at the market holds the certificate and scans the square on it with an ordinary phone. He gets a large banner: GENUINE — VALID. He sees the issuing laboratory, the certificate number and date, the sample number, the standard cited, a plain statement that the work was outside the accredited scope, and the customer's name masked. He sees not one measured value.

What the system doesThe code carries a seal only this laboratory can produce, so the check works with no internet at all. Where there is no signal the page says so honestly — signature verified, amendment status could not be checked — rather than implying the certificate is still current when it cannot know.
The mistake it preventsResults are never shown to an anonymous scan. A grade decides money in this cluster; to see it, the buyer needs the six-digit code printed on the paper, a one-time password to the mill's registered mobile, or a login of his own.
18

The silk goes home

Store Keeper

The forty sizing skeins, the twenty panels and the ten test pieces are gone, consumed in testing and recorded as consumed. The rest of the lot goes onto the retention shelf with a location and a date. In October Naidu comes to collect it and signs for it, and the handover is recorded with his name.

What the system doesIt sets the retain-until date from the retention policy for raw silk and puts the lot on the retention list. It also knows honestly how much material is left, so a retest request against silk that no longer exists returns "insufficient sample remaining" instead of a promise nobody can keep.
The mistake it preventsNothing is ever disposed of by itself. Disposal needs the Unit Incharge's authorisation, a reason, a date and, where the material has value, a witness — and the retention period for raw silk is one of the settings the specification asks the unit to confirm rather than assume.
And afterwardsCertificate DVM/GC/2026-27/00014 reaches the customer six days after the bales came across the counter, carrying the grade, the degrading arithmetic that produced it, the bale-2 caveat, and a code anyone can check. The mill can hand it to a buyer, and the buyer can satisfy himself it is genuine without telephoning the laboratory or asking anyone's favour. The laboratory, meanwhile, can now answer in seconds and from any desk the questions a paper register could never answer: which balance weighed sizing skein 27, who checked the arithmetic and at what hour, why cohesion was run twice, and what the customer was told about bale 2 before the work began. And if that balance ever fails a calibration, the system can list every result and every certificate it ever touched — which is the difference between a filing cabinet and a laboratory that can defend its own work.
What changed

Your ten points, and the six things we had to rethink

The ten points were the right ten, and every one of them survives in the specification. The note put the customer record first, saw that each sample needs its own identity that can be followed through the building, and said plainly that the tester should not know whose silk is on the bench and that nothing goes out before approval. It also asked for a code on the certificate so a holder can check the document without telephoning the unit — the exact instinct behind the verification page. And it named equipment calibration and consumable stock as work in their own right, not as filing to be done afterwards; the specification has taken that further rather than corrected it. What follows is not a list of mistakes. It is the set of questions a conversation does not have to answer and a specification does.

The invoice now follows the silk

The note saidThe note began with creating the customer and raising an invoice covering multiple tests, and only then receiving the samples.
What we do insteadWork now starts with a Test Request that a named officer formally reviews and accepts, followed by receipt and acceptance of the material. A quotation or an advance demand can still be given up front, but the tax invoice is raised at the end, for the work actually done.
Why it mattersThe charge usually cannot be known until the material has been counted and inspected, because the rate card charges by bale, by warp, by thousand cocoons and by measurement point, with a minimum of five skeins. Invoicing first makes cancelling and re-raising invoices a routine habit, which is precisely what a government audit objects to.
For exampleA customer books grading for about twenty bales. Eighteen arrive and one is torn open at the counter, so an invoice raised the day before is already wrong.

One job becomes sample plus test

The note saidThe note said one job per sample, so five samples would make five jobs.
What we do insteadThe specification keeps the sample as the physical silk, and adds a separate unit of work for each test on that sample. That unit of work is what gets assigned to a person, given a due date, and tied to one machine.
Why it mattersThe thing a Section Head actually hands to a tester is one test on one sample, not the sample as a whole. If the sample is the unit of work, four testers cannot share it, and per-test status, per-test due date and per-test equipment become impossible to record.
For exampleFive hanks, each needing conditioned mass, winding, size and size deviation, are five samples and twenty separate pieces of assignable work — not five jobs, and not one.

A result is many readings

The note saidThe note described a test log entry in which the tester records observations, results and remarks.
What we do insteadThe tester enters every original reading, one by one, and the system computes the average, the standard deviation, the coefficient of variation and the maximum deviation from them. A submitted reading is never overwritten; a correction keeps the original visible beside it, with who changed it, when and why.
Why it mattersThe original observations are what an assessor asks to see, because they are what allows the test to be repeated or the arithmetic re-checked years later. A single free-text box holds none of that, and cannot be re-computed if a formula is later corrected.
For exampleSize deviation on forty sizing skeins is forty separate weights kept individually, with the maximum deviation drawn from the four coarsest and four finest. On coarser silk the system asks for eighty, not forty, because the method says so.

Approval is two separate acts

The note saidThe note had a single step called test log approval, done by the authorised person.
What we do insteadFirst a competent second person technically verifies the readings, the arithmetic, the machine and the room conditions for one test. Then the Approving Authority authorises and signs the whole certificate. The verifier may only verify or send back with a reason — never edit a reading.
Why it mattersThese are two different acts, by two different people, with two different consequences, and merging them removes the check entirely. If the approver can quietly fix a figure, that figure is no longer attributable to the person who observed it.
For exampleA verifier who thinks a conditioned mass looks wrong sends it back with a reason; only the tester changes it, and the original figure stays visible. When the signatory is on tour, a dated delegation lets a named deputy sign, and the certificate prints the deputy's own name.

Issued certificates are amended, never edited

The note saidThe note ended at report generation and did not say what happens when a report has to change after it has gone out.
What we do insteadAn issued certificate can never be quietly re-typed. A correction is a separately numbered amendment or replacement that refers to the original, prints a mandatory reason for the change, and leaves the original retrievable and marked superseded.
Why it mattersThis is the single most common way a laboratory loses an audit, and a silently altered certificate is indefensible in a trade dispute. The unit's documents are exactly the kind that end up in such disputes, because money changes hands on a grade or a final kilogram figure.
For exampleA conditioning certificate goes out with the wrong number of bales, and the buyer already holds a printed copy. An amendment is issued, and the code on that old printed copy now reports that it has been superseded and names the document that replaces it.

The code proves the paper only

The note saidThe note asked that anyone scanning the code on the certificate should be able to view the report online.
What we do insteadAnyone scanning gets a plain verdict — genuine and current, genuine but superseded, genuine but withdrawn, or not genuine — with the laboratory, the report number, the date and the sample identity. The results themselves need a one-time password sent to the customer's registered number, or a login. Opening it fully is one setting, if the laboratory decides otherwise.
Why it mattersA grade or a size deviation decides payments in the local cluster, so a permanently open and guessable link would let anyone read a competitor's figures at leisure. Keeping the check public and the numbers protected preserves the whole benefit of the code without that exposure.
For exampleA buyer standing at the counter with a grading certificate can confirm in seconds that it is real and has not been superseded, without ever seeing another reeler's denier figures.

Most reports are not accredited

The note saidThe note assumed one report format for all work, and did not separate accredited from non-accredited testing.
What we do insteadThe seven entries of the accredited scope are loaded into the system with their validity dates, and only a report whose every parameter is inside those entries carries the accreditation symbol and the Unique Laboratory Report number. Where one sample carries both kinds of test, the system produces two separate certificates by itself.
Why it mattersOnly one of the seven entries is raw silk — Count, under IS 15090 (Part 5) — and five are fabric tests, so the plain report is the everyday document and the accredited one is the smaller stream. Marking a non-accredited test with an asterisk on an accredited report is not permitted, so the split cannot be left to a clerk to remember.
For exampleThe Limited Test, about 98 per cent of the unit's samples, is outside the accredited scope — the Unit In-Charge has confirmed it, and the status list he supplied marks it and twenty other items Non-NABL. None of the twenty-one maps to an accredited entry, so on today's catalogue every report is the plain one and the symbol appears nowhere. The accredited path is built, and waits behind a per-item flag.
Who uses it

Every person, and exactly what they see

The system shows each person only what their job needs. That is not merely tidy — it is the impartiality control your own note asked for, written into the software rather than left to habit.

Front Desk / Receipt ClerkDAILY

Meets the customer at the counter: finds the reeler by mobile number, raises the test request, checks the skeins in with their condition and photographs, prints the labels and the numbered acknowledgement slip, and takes the money.

Sees: A counter screen of seven tiles — awaiting sample, received today, registration incomplete, reports ready to hand over, enquiries pending, money received today by mode, and any notification that failed to go out.
Cannot: She cannot enter or change a single test reading, and she cannot waive a charge or alter a price on the rate card.

Tester / AnalystDAILY

Works the bench: scans the label, records sub-sample preparation and conditioning, enters the raw readings, attaches the instrument printout, notes the break causes, and submits.

Sees: His own queue in due-time order — say fourteen tests today, each shown as a sample number and a description such as 'Raw silk, 5 skeins, 20/22 D declared', with tiles for overdue work, tests sent back to him, specimens still conditioning, and controls due this run.
Cannot: He cannot change a reading once he has submitted it — a correction has to come back to him as a recorded send-back with a reason, which is exactly the point.

Verifier (technical checker)DAILY

Checks somebody else's submitted result before it can become a certificate: the readings, the arithmetic, the instrument and reagent status, the room conditions, and whether the number is plausible at all.

Sees: A verification queue ordered by ageing, and behind each row the full check screen — readings shown read-only with any excluded ones struck through and their reasons, the computed values with the formula and a Recompute button, the calibration and authorisation status as they stood at the moment of test, and a checklist that must be answered before the decision.

Blinding the tester — your idea, and its honest limits

Blinding hides the customer's identity from the person performing the test, so that knowing whose skeins these are cannot influence a reading. It is treated as a control on a real, documented impartiality risk — the laboratory names the risk, and the software produces the evidence that the control is working.

The tester sees the sample number, the material, and everything the customer declared — 'Raw silk, 5 skeins, 20/22 D declared' — because size deviation cannot be computed without the declared denier; those declared values are labelled as declared by the customer and not verified by the laboratory. He does not see the name, address, mobile, GSTIN, sender, the customer's own lot number, mark or chop, and no price, invoice, receipt or acknowledgement slip; the customer appears as a steady code such as CUST-4417, so staff can still discuss 'the 4417 lot' without knowing whose it is, and none of the hidden fields is merely hidden from view — it is never sent to his screen in the first place, nor printed on the bench worksheet, nor reachable by searching or exporting. If he genuinely needs the identity — the marks are ambiguous, the sample does not match its description, a mix-up is suspected — he requests a reveal with a reason from a fixed list, the Unit In-Charge approves it, and it opens for that one sample for a set window, sixty minutes by default.

Where it cannot be absolute

Blinding cannot be absolute here and the system must never be described as making it so: the filature's mark is stamped on the book, the tag is printed on the bale, the selvedge stamp is woven into the cloth, so the tester is quite literally holding the customer's identity in his hand. The answer is practical rather than perfect — at receipt the clerk transcribes the marks into a hidden field and over-labels the sample with the laboratory's own code, and where a mark cannot be covered she records 'identity not maskable', a count that is reported every month and is what the lab shows an assessor instead of a false claim. Every unblinding likewise is a request, a reason, a named approval and a line in the log reviewed monthly; and because CUST-4417 stays that customer's code, the monthly report states plainly that any single leak — one approved unblinding, one encounter at the counter, one unmaskable bale — resolves that customer's whole past and future history for that tester.

And what it will not fixThis system will not make a tensile test faster. It will not remove the need for discipline either: if a skein weight is written down wrongly the system will faithfully compute the wrong average, and if a reason for a change is typed as "correction" the record will say "correction" and mean nothing, so carelessness becomes visible and traceable but not harmless. And it will not run itself — it needs one named person in the unit who owns the master data (the test catalogue, the methods, the grade tables, the rate card, the holiday list), who keeps it right, and who has a trained deputy for when they are transferred. A laboratory system whose reference data has gone stale is worse than a register, because a register never pretends to be current.

If you don't answer, we will assume: You hold Section Head, Technical Manager and Report Writer alongside Unit In-Charge; the Verifier is a different person wherever staffing allows; and one named alternate signs during tour or leave.
OPEN-Q-A7
In a unit this size the same person will sometimes have to both perform a test and approve it. Should the software stop it, allow it and record the overlap, or allow it only with a second person's countersignature?
Why it matters: Claiming a separation in the quality manual that the staffing cannot actually deliver is worse than recording the overlap honestly.
If you don't answer, we will assume: Tester and checker must be different people as the rule, with any exception being your named, dated decision rather than a silent allowance, and a monthly count of how often it was used.
